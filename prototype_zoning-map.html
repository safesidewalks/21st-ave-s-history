The same block

Here’s what the city sees.

21st and 22nd Ave S. You’ve been here before. The streets haven’t changed. But there is a second set of decisions being made about this neighborhood — decisions that explain a lot about the first set.

Before January 21, 2026

Residential Small Lot.

RSL is Seattle’s “missing middle” zone — cottage-scale homes, small detached houses, the occasional duplex. One unit per every 2,000 sq ft of lot. Buildings are capped at 30 feet and 2,200 sq ft per unit. The city’s own description: “compatible in scale and character with existing houses.” These blocks had held this classification for decades.

January 21, 2026 · One Seattle Plan

Lowrise 1.

LR1 is the first tier of multifamily: townhouses, rowhouses, small apartment buildings. No cap on apartment units. Floor area ratio rises from 0.75 to 1.3 — nearly double the development capacity. The city’s own likely outcome: “Townhouses.” The sidewalk did not change.

Federal Opportunity Zone · Census Tracts 93 & 94

A federal invitation to investors.

These blocks sit inside a federally designated Opportunity Zone. Investors who develop here can defer and reduce capital gains taxes — a federal incentive designed to attract private capital to underinvested communities. The residents who have lived here, paid taxes here, and raised children here for six decades see none of that return.

During an April 2026 community walkthrough, an SDOT representative suggested that if residents did not accept the current proposal, the realistic alternative was to wait for developers to purchase properties and build sidewalks piecemeal.

In a historically redlined neighborhood that is now upzoned and inside a federal investor tax zone, that is not a description of how infrastructure sometimes gets built. It is a description of who the city thinks this neighborhood is for.
